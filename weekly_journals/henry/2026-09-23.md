# Week 6 Weekly Journal - Henry Smith

## What I Did

- Evaluated repetition coding and Reed-Solomon as possible recovery methods.
- Selected configurable repetition coding because it extends the current codec
  without adding an external dependency.
- Generalized the Week 5 three-copy format to support odd repetition factors
  from 3 through 15 while maintaining backward compatibility.
- Implemented and tested five-copy recovery, which can correct up to two
  removed or altered data symbols in each protected bit group.
- Added controlled 10%, 20%, and 30% removal and alteration tests, plus a 30%
  mixed-corruption test.
- Documented the implementation, test procedure, results, and limitations.

## What I Learned

- Increasing repetition from three to five copies raises the correction limit
  from one to two damaged symbols per bit group.
- An odd repetition factor guarantees that majority voting cannot tie when the
  group remains within its correction limit.
- Repetition coding is simple to integrate but increases message size and does
  not protect structural separators from deletion.

## Problems Encountered

- Percentage corruption must be distributed carefully. Concentrating three or
  more errors in one five-copy group exceeds the mathematical correction limit
  even when the overall corruption percentage is low.
- The method still depends on intact group separators. The decoder detects this
  structural damage and fails safely instead of returning corrupted text.
