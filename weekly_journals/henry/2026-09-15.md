# Week 5 Weekly Journal - Henry Smith

## What I Did

- Researched a simple repetition-code approach as the first recovery method for
  zero-width characters removed or altered during transfer.
- Added an optional three-copy recovery mode to the encoder and decoder.
- Added controlled tests showing successful recovery from one removed data
  symbol and one altered data symbol in a repeated group.
- Added a safe failure test for removed separators and documented the method's
  limits, overhead, and platform-testing considerations.

## What I Learned

- Majority voting can correct limited single-symbol damage when the boundaries
  between repeated groups remain intact.
- Error correction has tradeoffs: the recovery mode has more payload overhead
  and cannot fix every kind of platform normalization or removal.
- Checksum validation remains necessary because repetition alone cannot prove
  that all structural damage was corrected.

## Problems Encountered

- A platform that removes separators or all zero-width characters can still
  make the protected payload unrecoverable. The decoder reports this as a
  detected failure instead of returning unverified text.

## Next Week's Plan

- Review platform-returned samples with the team and compare normal versus
  recovery-mode behavior.
- Add regression tests for observed corruption patterns and evaluate whether a
  more advanced error-correction approach is appropriate.
