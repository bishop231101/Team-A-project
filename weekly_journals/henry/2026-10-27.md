## What I Learned

- Error correction cannot recover information when a platform removes the
  complete payload.
- Systematic removal of one symbol class is different from ordinary random bit
  substitution because the decoder loses bit positions and framing.
- Hamming SECDED is useful for controlled bit alterations, but its limitations
  must be stated clearly when platform evidence shows deletions.
- CRC validation remains necessary after error correction to prevent corrupted
  plaintext from being accepted.

## Challenges

- The available Gmail and Word results are preliminary, not official production
  trials, so they cannot support broad platform reliability claims.
- Neither Hamming nor Reed–Solomon directly fixes the observed complete or
  position-destroying stripping behavior.
